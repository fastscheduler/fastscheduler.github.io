KEY: help.admins_transfer.content
Transferring the Main Owner role is deliberately protected by multiple confirmation layers.

<b>The flow:</b>
1. The current Main Owner opens the target admin's profile and presses <b>Make Main Owner</b>.
2. The bot asks the owner to type a confirmation phrase <i>exactly</i> (GitHub-style), e.g. "I confirm that I transfer management of [channel] to [username]".
3. After the phrase is accepted, a final confirmation button appears — the transfer is not created until it's pressed.
4. A pending transfer request is sent to the target admin, who must <b>accept</b> or decline it.

<b>While pending:</b> the current owner can cancel the request at any time, and it expires automatically after <b>24 hours</b>.

<b>On accept:</b> the ownership swap is performed atomically — the target becomes the new Main Owner, the old owner stays as a regular admin with full permissions, and both parties are notified. At every critical step the bot re-validates that the target is still a real admin with Post Messages permission.

KEY: help.schedule_advanced.content
Beyond the basic flow, the scheduler covers a set of practical extras. Here is the complete picture.

<b>Editing scheduled messages</b>
Everything about a pending message stays editable until the moment it is sent: open the <b>Message List</b>, tap the message, and choose <b>🔄 Replace</b> (or use <code>/edit</code> with the job ID). You can change the text, the date and time, the attached media, and even the target channel. The bot shows a fresh preview before saving.

<b>Deleting messages</b>
Pending messages can be removed one by one from the Message List, via <code>/delete</code>, or by job ID. <b>Delete All</b> wipes every scheduled and recurring message for a channel at once — use it with care, the bot asks for confirmation first.

<b>Scheduling forwardable content</b>
Anything you can forward inside Telegram can be scheduled: forward a post from another channel while scheduling, and the bot copies its text and media into your own post. Polls and <b>quizzes</b> work the same way: create the poll in any chat and forward it to the bot during scheduling (or create it inline in the bot if your Telegram version supports poll sending), then pick a date. Only anonymous polls may post in channels — that is a Telegram rule for channels, not a bot limit.

<b>Special content types</b>
• <b>Location and venues</b> — send the location during scheduling.
• <b>Voice messages and video notes</b> — recorded or forwarded as usual.
• <b>Animated GIFs and stickers</b> — stickers can even carry a text caption, posted right after the sticker.

<b>Time zones</b>
Every date you enter is interpreted in the <b>channel's time zone</b>, so a channel with an international audience posts exactly when its readers are awake. Set or check it under <b>Channels</b> → the channel → <b>Time Zone</b>, or with <code>/timezone</code>.

KEY: help.connect_bot.content
Every channel needs its own sender bot - a Telegram bot <b>you</b> own that posts messages to your channel under the bot's name.

<b>Why Connect Your Own Bot?</b>
• Messages are sent from YOUR bot, not the main bot
• Your bot's name and photo appear as the author
• Professional appearance for your channel
• Low sending delays — your messages go out through your bot, not the shared main bot queue
• Detailed channel leaderboards — see most viewed, most reacted, and most commented posts for each channel

<b>How to Get a Bot Token</b>
1. Open <b>@BotFather</b> on Telegram
2. Send <code>/newbot</code> and follow the instructions
3. Choose a name and username for your bot
4. Copy the token (format: <code>1234567890:ABCdef..._XYZ</code>)
5. <b>Never share your token</b> with anyone you don't trust

<b>How to Connect</b>
1. In Fast Scheduler, click <b>Bots</b> or use <code>/bots</code>
2. Click <b>🔑 I Have a Token</b>
3. Send the bot token you copied from @BotFather
4. Select which channel this bot should send to
5. Make sure your bot is an <b>Administrator</b> in the target channel

<b>Limits</b>
• Free plan: 1 bot
• Premium: up to 3 bots

<b>Managing Bots</b>
• Click <b>Bots</b> on the main menu to see all connected bots
• You can replace a bot's token or disconnect it
• Disconnecting a bot puts its channel back into "connect a sender bot" state — reconnect a bot to schedule again

<b>Inside Your Sender Bot</b>
• Management moves to the sender bot automatically when it is connected
• <b>Management → Hide Channel</b> — strangers who start your bot won't see your channel name (Public Mode)
• <b>Management → Admins</b> — give other people limited access to your channel
• <b>Statistics</b> — open per-channel stats and leaderboards

<b>Troubleshooting</b>
• "Invalid token" — make sure you copied the FULL token from @BotFather
• "Bot is not admin" — add your bot as an administrator in the channel
• "Bot limit reached" — remove an existing bot or upgrade to Premium

KEY: help.formatting.content
You can use Telegram markdown-style formatting in your messages:

<code>**bold**</code> — <b>bold</b>
<code>__italic__</code> — <i>italic</i>
<code>___underline___</code> — <u>underline</u>
<code>~~strikethrough~~</code> — <s>strikethrough</s>
<code>||spoiler||</code> — <tg-spoiler>spoiler</tg-spoiler>
<code>`code`</code> — <code>inline code</code>
<code>```block```</code> — code block
<code>[text](url)</code> — <a href="https://example.com">text link</a>

<b>Note:</b> URLs in text are not automatically clickable in sent messages. Use inline buttons for clickable links.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
<b>Inline Buttons — Full Guide</b>
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Add clickable buttons below any post. There is no button-builder in the bot — just type the markup into your message text (usually at the end).

<b>Basic Syntax</b>
<code>&lt;inline&gt;Button Text&lt;link=&quot;https://url.com&quot;&gt;</code>

<b>Multiple Buttons (Each on its own row)</b>
<code>&lt;inline&gt;Buy Now&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com&quot;&gt;
&lt;inline&gt;Learn More&lt;link=&quot;https://info.com&quot;&gt;</code>

<b>Side by Side with --</b>
<code>&lt;inline&gt;Buy&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com&quot;&gt; -- &lt;inline&gt;Info&lt;link=&quot;https://info.com&quot;&gt;</code>
Result: [Buy] [Info] (same row)

<b>New Row with //</b>
<code>&lt;inline&gt;Catalog&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com&quot;&gt; // &lt;inline&gt;Support&lt;link=&quot;https://support.com&quot;&gt;</code>
Result:
[Catalog]
[Support]

<b>Full Layout Example</b>
<code>&lt;inline&gt;Catalog&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com&quot;&gt; -- &lt;inline&gt;Sale&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com/sale&quot;&gt; // &lt;inline&gt;Reviews&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com/reviews&quot;&gt; -- &lt;inline&gt;Support&lt;link=&quot;https://support.com&quot;&gt;</code>
Result:
[Catalog] [Sale]
[Reviews] [Support]

<b>Button Colors</b>
Add <code>style=&quot;success&quot;</code> (green), <code>style=&quot;primary&quot;</code> (blue) or <code>style=&quot;danger&quot;</code> (red) inside the tag:
<code>&lt;inline&gt;Buy Now&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com&quot; style=&quot;success&quot;&gt; -- &lt;inline&gt;Remove&lt;link=&quot;https://shop.com/remove&quot; style=&quot;danger&quot;&gt;</code>
Unknown styles are ignored.

<b>Important:</b>
• Use full URLs with <code>https://</code>
• Keep button text short (a few words) so it fits on phone screens
• The markup lines are removed from the published post — buttons appear BELOW the message text

<b>Examples</b>
• Link button: <code>&lt;inline&gt;Visit Site&lt;link=&quot;https://example.com&quot;&gt;</code>
• Side by side: <code>&lt;inline&gt;Like&lt;link=&quot;https://t.me/yourbot?start=like&quot;&gt; -- &lt;inline&gt;Dislike&lt;link=&quot;https://t.me/yourbot?start=dislike&quot;&gt;</code>

<b>Callback Buttons</b>
<code>&lt;inline&gt;Action Name&lt;callback="action_data"&gt;</code>

Callback buttons send data back to the bot instead of opening a link — use them for actions, confirmations, and interactive posts. They follow the same layout rules (<code>--</code> for the same row, <code>//</code> for a new row) and can be mixed with link buttons. Colors work here too: <code>&lt;inline&gt;Confirm&lt;callback=&quot;confirm&quot; style=&quot;success&quot;&gt;</code>.

<b>Important:</b>
• Callback data is limited to 64 bytes
• Malformed <code>&lt;inline&gt;</code> markup shows a warning — fix the syntax and resend
• Callback buttons only work when the bot recognizes the data — you'll be warned before scheduling if a callback has no matching handler
